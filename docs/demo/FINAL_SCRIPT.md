## 1. The problem (0:00.0–0:27.6)

Imagine you're importing cocoa from another country. Before you can confidently accept a shipment, you need to understand where that cocoa came from and whether the supporting land evidence is consistent. But that information can be spread across supplier spreadsheets, shipment documents, and satellite data. A shipment might look complete on paper while important details are missing or contradictory. That's the problem LotHold is built to help solve.

## 2. Introducing LotHold (0:27.6–0:46.2)

LotHold brings those records together into one review workflow. It helps cocoa exporters and importers organize shipment evidence, identify inconsistencies, examine available satellite observations, and document their decisions. Let me show you how it works.

## 3. Upload and verify documents (0:46.2–1:09.4)

First, I create a case for a shipment and upload the supplier records. LotHold reads the information and shows me exactly what it found. I can inspect the quantities, land references, and source files before accepting them. This matters because uploading a document doesn't automatically mean its information is correct. The reviewer stays in control.

## 4. Land evidence (1:09.4–1:37.0)

Next, LotHold examines published satellite data for the supplied locations. Here, we can see observations about tree-cover loss, including the years associated with those observations. These results help identify areas that may need closer investigation. But satellite observations alone don't prove that a supplier broke a law or that a shipment is non-compliant. They're evidence for a human reviewer to consider.

## 5. Findings and supplier request (1:37.0–2:00.9)

When LotHold finds a gap or a concern, it doesn't simply give the shipment a pass or fail. It shows the issue and links it back to the supporting information. The reviewer can then generate a request asking the supplier for the specific evidence needed. Instead of a vague request for more documents, the supplier gets a clearer explanation of what's missing.

## 6. Record the review (2:00.9–2:26.0)

Once the evidence has been examined, the reviewer records what they checked, their reasoning, and any supporting documents. LotHold keeps that decision connected to the evidence used during the review. If important information changes afterward, the previous decision may need to be reviewed again. This is about making the process easier to inspect, not automatically certifying the shipment.

## 7. XML draft and stale sign-off (2:26.0–2:48.4)

LotHold also supports preparing structured due-diligence records. A due-diligence statement is a formal declaration supported by the information a business has reviewed. Any generated XML is a draft for further validation and submission through the appropriate official process. It is not proof of regulatory acceptance.

## 8. Closing (2:48.4–3:14.2)

What you've seen is a real LotHold engineering test workflow, using clearly labelled test records rather than an actual cocoa shipment. The hosted pilot has demonstrated uploads, satellite analysis, PDF generation, and evidence recovery. Our goal is simple: make cocoa supply-chain evidence easier to organize, investigate, and review. This is LotHold.
